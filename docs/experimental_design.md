# Experiment design

1. State the hypothesis and distinguish observation from model-dependent forecasts.
2. Specify independent biological units (participants, sessions, trials) and split appropriately.
3. Fit standardization and dimensionality reduction on training data only.
4. Estimate reference trajectories on training recordings and evaluate on held-out trials.
5. Define perturbation magnitude, direction and latent-coordinate units.
6. Specify the ODE or fit and evaluate the predictive model against held-out persistence.
7. Predefine thresholds, time windows and outcome metrics.
8. Choose null controls consistent with exchangeability and temporal structure.
9. Report estimates, uncertainty, selection criteria and limitations without implying unsupported causality.

Selecting extreme deviations often yields an apparent later reduction under stationary noise. Use proper selection-matched controls; naive recovery fractions alone do not establish restoration.
